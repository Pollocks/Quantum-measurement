Units: the well width is L = 1 and the ground-state energy is E1 = 1. Thus En = n2, and time is measured in ℏ/E1. At 1× speed, dimensionless time τ = E1t/ℏ advances at 0.30 units per second of real time.
Position measurement: the result is sampled from the pre-measurement |ψ(x,t)|2. The post-measurement state is a Gaussian-like packet multiplied by sin(πx/L), so it is exactly zero at the walls. Δx controls its approximate width. The localized state is projected onto 60 box eigenstates.
Energy measurement: the result is sampled from the current eigenstate probabilities and collapses the system to that box eigenstate. Its |ψ|2 remains stationary while the clock continues to advance.
Initial state: Reset prepares a normalized superposition of the selected number of low-lying eigenstates. With 2 states, the default is the equal-weight, phase-aligned state (|1⟩+|2⟩)/√2.
Coefficient signs: The + and − symbols below the energy histogram record the signs of the real coefficients at the most recent preparation or measurement; coefficients generally become complex during subsequent time evolution. A small 0 indicates zero amplitude.
The finite 60-state basis is a deliberate pedagogical approximation, especially after a narrow position measurement.

1. State immediately after a position measurement

The measurement outcome x0 is randomly sampled from the probability density just before measurement. We prepare a narrow, real Gaussian-like wavefunction centered at x0:

ψ(x,t+)∝e−(x−x0)24σ2sin(πxL).

The sine factor makes the packet vanish at the walls (x = 0 and x = L). The parameter σ sets the packet width; the simulation normalizes the resulting state.

2. Expansion in the energy eigenbasis

ψ(x,t+)≈∑n=1Ncnϕn(x),

where the infinite-well eigenfunctions are

ϕn(x)=2Lsin(nπxL).

The expansion coefficients are calculated by projection:

cn=∫0Lϕn(x)ψ(x,t+)dx.

Because both functions in the integrand are real, every coefficient cn is real immediately after measurement, although some may be negative. Their phases are therefore 0 or π, relative to the chosen real eigenfunctions.

3. Subsequent Schrödinger evolution

ψ(x,t)=∑n=1Ncne−iEn(t−t+)/ℏϕn(x).

Relative phases develop, so |ψ(x,t)|² changes with time, while each energy probability Pn = |cn|² remains constant until another measurement.

4. Energy measurement

Measuring energy gives En = n²E1 with probability |cn|² and leaves the particle in the corresponding eigenstate. Its probability density is then stationary, although the phase and the simulation clock continue evolving.

Numerical approximation: The simulation uses L = 1 and a finite basis of N = 60 states. The localized packet is projected numerically onto this basis and renormalized. Consequently, very narrow packets are approximate rather than exact Gaussians.
